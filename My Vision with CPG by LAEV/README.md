# LAEV's Vision

## Chain Poker Genesis Physical Digital Table

When I imagine Chain Poker Genesis, I do not imagine only a protocol, an application, or a platform for playing poker.

I imagine a physical scene.

I imagine a table.

A real table, where several people are sitting face to face, playing poker live. I can imagine the atmosphere of a cash game, the conversations, the decisions, the tension of every hand, and the interaction between the players. But at the same time, I imagine that this table has something that a traditional table does not have: **it is a piece of digital infrastructure for Chain Poker Genesis**.

For me, that is an important part of what I want to build.

I do not want to eliminate the physical experience of poker.

I want to bring digital infrastructure directly into it.

***

## I Imagine Arriving at the Table with My Own Node

One of the things that interests me most about this idea is that the player does not have to arrive at the table and create an account on a computer that belongs to someone else.

I want to imagine something different.

I imagine that every player can have their own **Chain Poker Genesis Node Computer Laptop**.

A dedicated laptop, or a laptop prepared to operate as their node.

The player carries that computer with them.

It is their infrastructure.

It is where they can maintain their node, their identity, their information, and the components required to participate in Chain Poker Genesis.

Then I imagine arriving at a physical table.

I sit down.

And instead of simply registering in a centralized application, I connect my own node to the table.

```text
PLAYER
   │
   ▼
CHAIN POKER GENESIS NODE LAPTOP
   │
   ▼
PHYSICAL DIGITAL TABLE
```

For me, that image represents something important:

**the player carries their own node to the network.**

***

## I Imagine Real Players, with Their Own Nodes, Sitting at the Same Table

I imagine a table where eight players, for example, are physically present.

They are not avatars.

They are not simply accounts.

They are people.

Each one may have arrived with their own portable node.

```text
        PLAYER NODE
             │
             ▼
       ┌───────────┐
       │  TABLE    │
       │   NODE    │
       └───────────┘
             ▲
             │
        PLAYER NODE
```

Each player connects their node to the table infrastructure.

The table then becomes more than simply a place where cards and chips are located.

It becomes a point where different personal nodes can meet and operate together.

That is part of what I want to explore with Chain Poker Genesis.

***

## The Physical Table

When I think about the table, I still imagine a poker table.

Not a robotic machine.

Not a table that tries to replace people.

I want a table where the player's physical presence still exists.

But I want it to be digitally integrated.

In front of each player, I imagine an interface, tablet, or display integrated into their position.

That interface allows the player to see their private cards and make their decisions:

**Fold.  
Check.  
Call.  
Bet.  
Raise.  
All-in.**

At the center of the table, I imagine a larger screen.

It can represent the public state of the game:

**Flop.  
Turn.  
River.  
Pot.  
Blinds.  
Dealer.  
Bets.  
Player to act.**

This creates a natural separation between what belongs to the player and what belongs to the public space of the game.

***

## I Want My Node to Remain Mine

This part is fundamental to my vision.

If I bring my Chain Poker Genesis Node Computer Laptop to a table, I do not want the table to become the owner of my node.

My node remains mine.

My identity remains mine.

The table simply provides a physical environment where I can use my node.

That is why I imagine this relationship:

```text
MY NODE
   │
   │
   ▼
TABLE SESSION
   │
   ▼
GAME
```

When I finish playing, I get up and take my node with me.

The table remains where it is.

Another player can sit down afterward.

But my identity and my infrastructure do not remain stored in that seat as if they belonged to the table.

***

## The Hardware Wallet

I also imagine that the physical connection can be complemented by a **Hardware Wallet / HD Wallet**.

The laptop can run the node, while the Hardware Wallet can provide another layer of security and authorization.

I can imagine the player arriving with their portable node and their physical wallet.

They connect their node to the table.

They connect the Hardware Wallet through USB.

And they physically perform the corresponding authorization.

```text
PLAYER
  │
  ├── NODE LAPTOP
  │
  └── HARDWARE WALLET
           │
           ▼
      AUTHORIZATION
           │
           ▼
        TABLE
```

For me, this creates an interesting relationship between the physical and digital worlds.

The person is physically present.

Their node is physically present.

Their custody hardware can also be physically present.

And the table becomes the point where these elements interact.

***

## The Table as a Node Channel

This is where my idea begins to go beyond poker.

When I imagine several connected tables, I begin to see them as something similar to **physical channels of a node network**.

A table can represent in the network the players who are physically connected to it.

```text
                 NETWORK
                    │
          ┌─────────┴─────────┐
          │                   │
       TABLE A             TABLE B
       NODE                 NODE
      / |  \               / |  \
     /  |   \             /  |   \
   P1  P2   P3           P4  P5   P6
```

Each player maintains their own node.

Each table maintains its own node.

And the table can function as a **Node Channel** connecting these nodes to the Chain Poker Genesis infrastructure.

This is one of the reasons I am so interested in starting with a physical table.

Because a poker table can become a tangible demonstration of a larger concept.

***

## I Do Not Want Every Node to Have to Be Directly Exposed to the Internet

Another reason I imagine this architecture is that I do not necessarily want every player to have to maintain a public server that is permanently exposed.

The player can carry their node.

The table can provide the necessary connectivity.

The table can connect to the Internet.

The Table Node can act as a communication point between the local network of the table and the external infrastructure.

So I can imagine something like this:

```text
PLAYER NODE
     │
     │
     ▼
TABLE NODE
     │
     │
     ▼
INTERNET
     │
     ▼
CHAIN POKER GENESIS NETWORK
```

This also allows me to imagine a **local-first** model, where the fundamental interaction of the game takes place within the table itself and the external connection is used for the functions that actually need to leave that environment.

***

## The Game Remains Physical

There is something I do not want to lose sight of.

Even though the infrastructure is digital, the game still takes place between people.

I imagine players looking at each other.

Talking.

Waiting.

Making decisions.

Watching how the atmosphere changes when the flop appears.

Seeing another player's reaction to a raise.

And at the same time, the entire digital state of the game is being managed by Chain Poker Genesis infrastructure.

That contrast is precisely what interests me.

**A completely physical human experience supported by digital and decentralized infrastructure.**

***

## The Table as a Collective Computer, Without Taking the Player's Node Away

The table can have its own Table Node.

That node coordinates the game.

It can maintain the state of the table, positions, hands, game transitions, and communication with the interfaces.

But I do not want to confuse it with the player's node.

I imagine it like this:

```text
PLAYER 1 NODE ─┐
PLAYER 2 NODE ─┤
PLAYER 3 NODE ─┤
PLAYER 4 NODE ─┤
               ▼
          TABLE NODE
               │
               ▼
       CHAIN POKER GENESIS
```

The Table Node coordinates the environment.

The Player Nodes represent the players.

The network allows both to work together.

***

## I Imagine a Live Broadcast

I also imagine this table as a physical platform for live broadcasts.

Cameras around the table.

An in-house production.

Graphics.

Real-time data.

The public state of the game.

And a broadcast for people watching from anywhere else.

But for me, there is a fundamental distinction:

**the broadcast should not be the authority of the game.**

The broadcast represents what is happening.

The protocol and the verifiable state of the game are something else.

I can imagine:

```text
TABLE NODE
   │
   ├── GAME STATE
   │
   ├── PUBLIC STATE
   │
   └── MEDIA DATA
          │
          ▼
      LIVE STREAM
```

In this way, the same table can simultaneously be a gaming environment and an audiovisual production point.

***

## I Imagine the Player Being Able to Take Their Node to Different Tables

This is another part that I particularly like.

I imagine a person having their **Chain Poker Genesis Node Computer Laptop** and using it in different places.

Today they can be at one table.

Tomorrow at another.

They can travel with their node.

They can connect it to compatible infrastructure.

The table changes.

The player does not necessarily have to change their identity.

```text
              PLAYER NODE
                   │
       ┌───────────┼───────────┐
       ▼           ▼           ▼
    TABLE A     TABLE B     TABLE C
```

The physical infrastructure changes.

The player's identity can remain.

***

## What I Am Really Imagining

When I bring all these ideas together, I realize that the physical table is only one part of something larger.

I am imagining an architecture where:

**each person can have their own node;**

**each physical infrastructure can have its own node;**

**and channels allow those nodes to meet and work together.**

Chain Poker Genesis would then be the initial environment where this idea can become tangible.

The poker table would be one of the first physical forms of this concept.

The player carries the node.

The table provides the channel.

The Table Node coordinates.

The Hardware Wallet can authorize.

The network connects.

And the game happens physically between people.

***

I think about demonstrating that a decentralized network can also have **physical presence**.

I imagine a table where a person can arrive with their own portable node, connect it to physical infrastructure, and, within moments, become part of a real game with other people who have done exactly the same thing.

One table.

Several players.

Several personal nodes.

A Table Node.

A channel.

A network.

And people playing face to face.

For me, that is one of the clearest images of what **Chain Poker Genesis by LAEV** can represent:

> **A decentralized network that does not exist only on the Internet, but can also manifest physically wherever its nodes meet.**

And the Chain Poker Genesis table can be one of those places.
